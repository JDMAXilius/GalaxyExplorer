# Milky Way destinations

The nine labels on the galaxy map. Two of them switch experience (Solar System,
Galactic Center) and use the panels in `experiences.md`; the seven below open as
overlays with their own panel: title and two paragraphs. ASCII only.

Each also carries a **Second line**, set in caps under the name on its map tag.
It answers "what am I about to go and look at" in one line - what kind of object
it is and how far away - which is the treatment the two inherited markers used
and the one every tag now uses. It is not repeated in the panel, which says the
same things at length.

## helix
**Title:** Helix Nebula
**Second line:** Planetary nebula - 650 light-years

A Sun-like star at the end of its life has shrugged off its outer layers,
leaving a glowing shell of gas around the white dwarf that remains. We happen to
see the shell close to face-on, which is why it looks like an eye.

At 650 light-years it is one of the nearest planetary nebulae, and about two and
a half light-years wide. Our own Sun will end much the same way.

What surrounds you is an impression built from the Hubble photograph of the
nebula, wrapped around you so you can see its colours at size. The real shell
is two and a half light-years across and we see it from the outside, face-on;
nobody has been inside it.

## crab
**Title:** Crab Nebula
**Second line:** Supernova remnant - 6,500 light-years

The wreckage of a star that exploded in 1054, bright enough that Chinese and
Japanese astronomers recorded seeing it in daylight for more than three weeks.

At its centre spins a neutron star the size of a city, turning thirty times a
second. Its beam sweeps the whole cloud and keeps it glowing nearly a thousand
years later.

What surrounds you is an impression built from the photograph, projected
around you so the filaments can be seen at size. In reality this is nine
light-years of expanding debris viewed from four thousand light-years away,
and no camera has ever been within it.

## homunculus
**Title:** Homunculus Nebula
**Second line:** Eruption cloud - 7,500 light-years

Two lobes of gas thrown out by Eta Carinae, a colossal double star, during an
eruption in the 1840s that briefly made it the second-brightest star in the sky.

The star survived what should have killed it. It remains one of the most massive
and unstable stars known, and is expected to end as a supernova.

What surrounds you is an impression built from the photograph of the two
lobes, opened out around you. The real Homunculus is a shell of ejecta about a
light-year across wrapped close around Eta Carinae, and this is a way of
showing it rather than a view from within.

---

## orion
**Title:** Orion Nebula
**Second line:** Star-forming region - 1,344 light-years

The closest large nursery of new stars, visible without a telescope as the fuzzy
middle point in the sword of Orion.

A cluster of hot young stars lights the gas from inside, while hundreds of
infant solar systems take shape in the shadows around them. This is roughly what
our own system looked like four and a half billion years ago.

What surrounds you is an impression built from the photograph, projected onto
the sky around you. The nebula really is a cloud you could be inside -
twenty-four light-years of it - but this is that photograph arranged around
you, not a view recorded from in there.

---

## pillars
**Title:** Pillars of Creation
**Second line:** Star-forming region - 6,500 light-years

Towers of cold gas and dust several light-years tall, standing in the Eagle
Nebula and sculpted by radiation pouring off young stars nearby.

New stars are condensing inside the columns while the same radiation eats them
away from outside. Hubble's photograph of them in 1995 became one of the most
recognised images in astronomy.

What surrounds you is an impression built from the Hubble and Webb
photographs, projected around you so the columns stand at size. They are four
to five light-years tall and are genuinely something you could be among; no
camera has been.

---

## ngc1501
**Title:** NGC 1501
**Second line:** Planetary nebula - 4,240 light-years

A planetary nebula nicknamed the Oyster: a rippling bubble of gas blown off by a
dying star, about a light-year across.

The exposed stellar core at its centre pulses in brightness every half hour,
which makes it one of the more closely watched objects of its kind.

What surrounds you is an impression built from the photograph, opened out
around you so its structure can be seen. The real nebula is about a third of a
light-year across - a small, compact object seen from outside - so this is a
way of looking closely, not a place.

---

## trumpler14
**Title:** Trumpler 14
**Second line:** Star cluster - 8,980 light-years

One of the youngest and most crowded star clusters known, packed into the Carina
Nebula about nine thousand light-years away.

Its thousands of stars are only a few hundred thousand years old, and the
brightest of them are among the most luminous in the galaxy. They will burn out
quickly and explode.

What surrounds you is an impression built from the photograph of the Carina
Nebula that holds this cluster, wrapped around you. The cloud is a place you
could be inside; the arrangement here, and the stars scattered through it, are
ours rather than measured.
